Privacy Policy

Effective date: 10 November 2026 · Last updated: 10 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how the Kaihua mobile app for iOS and Android (the “App”) handles information. The App is developed and published by Shuyelab (“we”, “us”). If you have any question, write to shuyelab@gmail.com.

The short version
  • There is no account and no sign-up. We do not run a server that receives your data.
  • Your learning progress and settings are stored only on your device. Uninstalling the App deletes them.
  • Purchases are processed by Apple or Google. RevenueCat helps us check whether you have Kaihua Pro.
  • Users without Pro may see ads from Google AdMob. Ads never appear while you study. In regions where consent is required, Google’s consent form lets you choose how ads use your data. On iPhone, ads are personalised with your advertising identifier only if you allow tracking in Apple’s prompt.
  • We do not sell your personal information.

1. Information stored on your device

To work, the App keeps the following data in a local database on your device:

  • learning progress: which words you have studied, your answers, review history and the spaced-repetition (FSRS) schedule;
  • settings: explanation language, starting level, daily study time, new words per day, voice, reminder on/off;
  • exam plan: the HSK exam date and target level you choose;
  • Tone Camp progress and scores, streak information;
  • your age group and, only if you answer the optional onboarding question, how you heard about Kaihua (for example “YouTube” or “a friend”).

This data is not sent to us or to any server operated by us. The App currently has no cloud sync, so this data is not backed up by us and does not move to a new phone automatically. Deleting the App deletes this data. If we add an optional sync feature in the future (for example via iCloud), we will update this policy before it ships.

2. Information we collect

We do not collect personal information through the App. We do not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or precise location.

If you contact us by email, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message. We use it only to answer you and to fix problems you report, and we delete the conversation within 12 months after it ends unless we need to keep it for a legal reason.

3. Third-party services

The App uses a small number of third-party services. They receive data directly from the App under their own privacy policies.

3.1 Apple App Store and Google Play

All payments are handled by Apple or Google. We never see your card or bank details. We receive sales reports from the stores that do not identify you personally.

3.2 RevenueCat (in-app purchases)

We use RevenueCat, Inc. to validate purchases and to unlock Kaihua Pro, including when you tap “Restore purchases”. RevenueCat receives a random, anonymous app user ID that it creates, your purchase history and store transaction information (product, price, currency, store country, purchase and renewal dates), and basic technical information such as platform, OS and app version. This is not linked to your name or email because the App has no account. Purpose: app functionality (unlocking what you paid for, preventing fraud) and purchase analytics. Privacy policy: revenuecat.com/privacy.

3.3 Google AdMob (ads, only for users without Pro)

The App shows ads in only two places: a rewarded video that you choose to watch (to unlock 5 extra new words for the day, or to repair a streak), and one ad on the “session complete” screen (a native ad, or a banner if no native ad is available). No ads are shown during a study session, there are no interstitial ads, and Kaihua Pro users see no ads. We limit ad content to the “Teen” (T) maturity rating or below, and the App is not marked as directed to children.

According to Google, the Google Mobile Ads SDK automatically collects and shares:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of your device;
  • product interactions, such as app launches, taps and video views, and information about the ads shown;
  • diagnostic and performance information, such as crash logs, app launch time and hang rate;
  • device identifiers: on Android, the advertising ID and app set ID; on iOS, app- or developer-scoped identifiers and, only if you allow tracking (see 3.5), the iOS advertising identifier (IDFA).

Google uses this data to show and measure ads, for analytics and to prevent fraud. Whether ads are personalised depends on your consent choices, your device settings, your age group (see section 6) and local law. Data is encrypted in transit. Learn more: Google Privacy Policy · How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

3.4 Google User Messaging Platform (consent)

Before any ad is requested, the App uses Google’s User Messaging Platform (UMP) to check whether a privacy message is required where you are — for example a consent form in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, or an opt-out message under some US state privacy laws. Ads are only requested if the result allows it. Where required, you can change your choice at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices.

3.5 Apple App Tracking Transparency (iPhone and iPad)

On iOS, after the consent step above, the App shows Apple’s “Allow tracking?” prompt (not shown to users identified as under 18, see section 6). If you tap Allow, Google may use your device’s advertising identifier (IDFA), together with the data listed in 3.3, to personalise ads and measure them across apps and websites owned by other companies — this is “tracking” as defined by Apple. If you tap Ask App Not to Track, the IDFA is not available and ads are not personalised with it. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking → Kaihua. Your study progress is never used for ads and never leaves your device.

5. AI-generated content

Some audio in the App (text-to-speech) and some text (meanings, example sentences and memory hooks) is generated with AI. Before release, this content is cross-checked by several AI models, checked automatically against reference data such as the official HSK syllabus, and reviewed by people, including native speakers for sampled entries. Memory hooks are mnemonics, not the historical origin (etymology) of a character. The content is created before the App is published; the App does not send your data to any AI service. If you spot an error, please tell us at shuyelab@gmail.com.

6. Children

Kaihua is intended for people aged 13 and over. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly provide the service to, or collect personal information from, children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 is using the App, contact us and we will help remove any related data held by our service providers.

Teens (13–17). When the App knows that a user is 13–17 (from the age question in the App’s setup), it handles ads more strictly: ads are requested as non-personalised, the Google consent request is marked as coming from a user under the age of consent, ad requests are flagged for age-restricted treatment, and the iOS tracking prompt is not shown, so the IDFA is never accessed. Where no age has been given, the App follows the general rules described in section 3 and the consent result collected through Google’s UMP, including any higher age of digital consent set by local law (for example up to 16 in some EU countries).

7. How long data is kept

  • On-device data: until you delete it or uninstall the App.
  • RevenueCat: for as long as needed to provide purchase validation and as described in RevenueCat’s policy. We can ask RevenueCat to delete a purchase record on your request.
  • Google AdMob: as described in Google’s policies.
  • Emails to us: up to 12 months after the conversation ends.

8. Security

Data sent to RevenueCat and Google is encrypted in transit (TLS). On-device data is protected by your operating system’s app sandbox. No method of storage or transmission is 100% secure, but we keep the data we handle to a minimum.

9. International transfers

RevenueCat and Google process data in the United States and other countries. They state that they use appropriate safeguards, such as the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses, for international transfers.

10. Your choices and rights

  • On-device data: you control it fully. Uninstalling the App deletes it.
  • Ads: change consent in Settings → Ad privacy choices (where shown). On iPhone, turn tracking off in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in system settings. Buying Pro removes ads.
  • Purchase records: email us and include your store order ID (Apple: from your purchase receipt email; Google: “GPA.…”) so we can find the anonymous RevenueCat record and ask for it to be deleted. Your purchase history with Apple or Google is managed by them.
  • Notifications: turn them off in Settings or in system settings at any time.

European Economic Area, United Kingdom and Switzerland (GDPR / UK GDPR)

Legal bases: performance of a contract (validating and providing purchases), consent or legitimate interests as applicable (ads, collected through UMP), and legitimate interests (security and fraud prevention). You have the right to access, correct, delete, restrict or object to processing, to data portability, and to withdraw consent at any time without affecting earlier processing. You may also complain to your local data protection authority.

United States (including California CCPA/CPRA)

We do not sell personal information for money. Ads delivered through Google AdMob may involve “sharing” of identifiers and internet activity for cross-context behavioural advertising as defined by some state laws. You can opt out using the privacy message shown by Google’s UMP where available (later via Settings → Ad privacy choices), by not allowing tracking on iOS, by deleting or resetting your advertising ID on Android, or by buying Pro. We will not discriminate against you for exercising your rights. Categories involved: identifiers (device IDs), internet or network activity (ad interactions), approximate location derived from IP address, and commercial information (purchase history).
