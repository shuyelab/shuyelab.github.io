Privacy Policy

Effective date: 13 November 2026 · Last updated: 13 November 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how the Kaihua mobile app for iPhone and Android (the “App”) handles information. The App is published by Shuye Lab (also written “Shuyelab”; “we”, “us”), the studio name of Kaihua’s individual developer, who is the seller shown on the App Store and Google Play and is located in mainland China. Questions: shuyelab@gmail.com.

The short version
  • There is no account and no sign-up. We run no server of our own, and the App never asks for your name, email address or phone number.
  • Your learning progress and settings are stored only on your device. Uninstalling the App deletes them from the device.
  • Purchases are processed by Apple or Google. RevenueCat checks whether you have Kaihua Pro or Remove Ads, using a random app user ID instead of your name or email.
  • Users without Pro or Remove Ads may see ads from Google AdMob, but never while studying. Google collects device and usage data to show and measure ads. Where the law requires consent, Google’s consent form asks you first; on iPhone, your advertising identifier is used only if you allow tracking.
  • The App asks your age group once, without asking for a birth date. Users under 13 see no ads and users aged 13–17 see only non-personalised ads; the threshold is 14 when your device region is Korea or your phone language is Korean (section 5).
  • We do not sell your personal information for money. How ad data is treated under US state laws is explained in section 11.

3. What we see and what we collect ourselves

We do not run our own servers, analytics or crash-reporting tools, and the App does not ask for your name, email address, phone number, contacts, photos or precise location. The services built into the App (section 4) do collect some data. Of that data, we can see purchase and transaction records in RevenueCat’s dashboard, listed by the random app user ID, and summary reports from Google about ads. We do not try to identify you from them.

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you include (for example a screenshot or a store order ID). We use it only to reply and to fix problems you report. Our mailbox is hosted by Google (Gmail). We delete the conversation within 12 months after it ends, unless the law requires us to keep it longer.

4. Third-party services

The App uses the following services. They receive data directly from your device.

4.1 Apple App Store and Google Play

All payments are handled by Apple or Google. We never see your card or bank details. The stores give us sales reports that do not identify you. Your purchase history with the store is kept by Apple or Google under their own policies.

4.2 RevenueCat (purchases)

RevenueCat, Inc. checks which purchases you have (Kaihua Pro or Remove Ads) when the App starts, when you buy and when you tap Restore purchases. This happens for every user, including users who never buy anything. RevenueCat receives:

  • a random app user ID created on your device by RevenueCat’s software;
  • your purchase history and store transaction information (product, price, currency, store country, purchase and renewal dates, the store receipt or purchase token);
  • technical information such as device type, operating system, app version, the time the App was last used, and the IP address used for the connection.

We do not send RevenueCat your name, email address or any other personal details. The app user ID is not your name, but together with your IP address and purchase details it may still count as personal data under some laws. Purpose: providing what you paid for, restoring purchases, preventing fraud, and purchase statistics for us. RevenueCat stores this data on Amazon Web Services in the United States. Privacy policy: revenuecat.com/privacy.

4.3 Google AdMob (ads)

Ads are shown only to users who have neither Kaihua Pro nor Remove Ads, and only when their age group allows ads (section 5). They appear in two places: a rewarded video that you choose to watch (for 5 extra new words that day, or to repair a one-day break in your streak), and one ad on the “session complete” screen (a native ad, or a banner if no native ad is available). No ads are shown during a study session, and there are no pop-up (interstitial) ads. Users with Pro or Remove Ads, and users under the age threshold, are not offered rewarded videos either. Ad content is limited to Google’s “T” (teen) rating or below. The App is not tagged as directed to children.

According to Google, the Google Mobile Ads SDK automatically collects and shares the following for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention:

  • your IP address, which may be used to estimate the general location of your device;
  • product interactions, such as app launches, taps and video views, and information about the ads shown;
  • diagnostic and performance information, such as crash logs, app launch time, hang rate and energy use;
  • device identifiers: on Android, the advertising ID and app set ID; on iPhone, identifiers limited to the app or developer, and the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking (section 4.5).

On Android devices that support it, the SDK may also use Android’s Privacy Sandbox ad features (Topics and attribution reporting), which you can turn off in your phone’s ad privacy settings.

Whether ads are personalised depends on your consent choices, your device settings, your age group and local law. Outside regions where Google asks for consent, Google may show personalised ads to adults unless you opt out in your device settings (section 9). Non-personalised ads still use your IP address, device and interaction information to deliver and measure the ad, limit how often it is shown and prevent fraud. Data is encrypted in transit. Learn more: Google Privacy Policy · How Google uses information from apps that use its services.

4.4 Google User Messaging Platform (ad consent)

Before any ad is requested, the App asks Google’s User Messaging Platform (UMP) whether a privacy message is required where you are, for example a consent form in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, or an opt-out message under some US state privacy laws. To work this out, Google receives your IP address and device information, and it stores your choice. Ads are requested only if the result allows it. Where a choice is required, you can change it at any time in Settings → Ad privacy choices.

4.5 Apple App Tracking Transparency (iPhone)

On iPhone, after the consent step above, users aged 18 or over see Apple’s “Allow tracking?” prompt. If you tap Allow, Google may use your IDFA together with the data in section 4.3 to personalise ads and measure them across apps and websites owned by other companies, which Apple calls “tracking”. If you tap Ask App Not to Track, the IDFA is not available to the App or to Google; ads may still be shown using the other data in section 4.3. You can change this at any time in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. Your study progress is never used for ads and never leaves your device.

5. Age groups and children

Kaihua is not directed to children under 13 (under 14 in Korea). The App does not collect information that identifies a person, such as a name or email address, and there are no accounts.

During first-time setup the App asks one neutral question, “How old are you?”, with three answers. No birth date is asked for or stored, and the answer stays on your device. The App does not ask for ad consent or request any ad until this question has been answered. The age threshold is 14 if your device region is set to Korea or your phone language is Korean, and 13 otherwise.

  • Under 13 (under 14 when the Korean threshold applies): the App suggests studying with a parent or guardian and turns ads off completely. It does not start Google’s ads service, ask for ad consent, request any ad, offer rewarded videos or show the tracking prompt.
  • 13–17 (14–17): ads are requested as non-personalised, Google’s consent request is marked as coming from a user under the age of consent, ad requests are flagged for age-restricted treatment, and the iPhone tracking prompt is not shown, so the IDFA is never accessed.
  • 18 or older: the general rules in section 4 apply.

For users aged 13–17, Google therefore receives a signal that the user is under the age of consent and should get age-restricted ad treatment. The App never sends an age, a birth date or the answer itself.

The answer is self-declared and we cannot verify it. For every age group, RevenueCat still checks purchases with the random app user ID described in section 4.2. Purchases are made through the store, where parental controls such as Apple’s “Ask to Buy” and Google Play’s purchase approval apply.

If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has given us personal information (for example by emailing us), contact shuyelab@gmail.com. We will delete it, and if you send us the store order ID we will ask RevenueCat to delete the related purchase record.

7. Content made with AI (legal notice)

The audio pronunciations in the App (text-to-speech) and much of its text (meanings, example sentences, grammar explanations, memory hooks, topic labels and short meanings for words outside the course) are generated with AI. The word lists themselves come from the official HSK 3.0 syllabus and are not generated. Before release, this content is cross-checked by several AI models and checked automatically against the official HSK syllabus and reference dictionaries, in several rounds. Memory hooks are mnemonics, not the historical origin (etymology) of a character. All of this content is made before the App is published; the App does not send your information to any AI service. If you spot an error, please tell us at shuyelab@gmail.com. This notice is given as required by Korea’s AI Basic Act (Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of Trust).

8. How long data is kept

  • On-device data: until you uninstall the App (copies in your own device backups follow your backup settings).
  • RevenueCat: for as long as needed to check and restore purchases and to meet legal obligations, under our agreement with RevenueCat. We can ask RevenueCat to delete a purchase record on your request.
  • Google AdMob and UMP: as described in Google’s policies.
  • Emails to us: up to 12 months after the conversation ends.

When the retention period ends, we delete emails permanently. Data held by RevenueCat or Google is deleted under their own procedures.

9. Your choices and rights

  • On-device data: fully under your control. Uninstall the App to delete it from the device.
  • Ads: change your consent in Settings → Ad privacy choices (shown where a choice is required). On iPhone, turn tracking off in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. On Android, delete or reset your advertising ID and manage ad privacy features in your phone’s settings (usually Settings → Privacy → Ads). Buying Pro or Remove Ads removes ads. These choices affect future processing; to ask Google about data it already holds, use the tools linked from the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Purchase records: email us with your store order ID (Apple: in your receipt email; Google: starts with “GPA.”) so we can find the RevenueCat record and ask for it to be deleted. See how to delete your data.
  • Notifications: turn them off in Settings or in your system settings at any time.
  • Emails: ask us to see, correct or delete the emails you sent us.

You can also ask us to access, correct or delete personal information, restrict or object to its use, receive a copy, or withdraw consent. Because we hold almost nothing ourselves, most requests are handled by pointing you to the settings above or by passing a deletion request to RevenueCat. We reply within 15 business days, or sooner where the law requires it (for example within 10 days for requests under Korea’s PIPA). We will not treat you differently for using these rights.

Security: data sent to RevenueCat and Google is encrypted in transit (TLS). On-device data is protected by your operating system’s app sandbox. Only the developer can read the support mailbox. No method of storage or transmission is completely secure, so we keep the data we handle to a minimum.
